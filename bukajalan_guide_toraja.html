Introduction

Why Toraja surprised us completely

We had heard about Toraja before our first visit, but nothing quite prepares you for the reality of it. The highlands of South Sulawesi feel like a world apart — lush, green, cool, and filled with a culture that has no real comparison anywhere else in Indonesia.

The Torajan people are known above all for their elaborate funeral ceremonies — events that can last days, involve hundreds of guests, and hold a deep spiritual significance. It sounds unusual on paper. In person, it is one of the most moving things we have ever witnessed. You will understand when you get there, and we cannot wait for you to experience it.

Getting there

How to reach Toraja

Most visitors fly into Makassar (Sultan Hasanuddin Airport) and then take a shared bus or private car to Rantepao — the main town in Toraja. The journey takes around 8–9 hours through increasingly dramatic landscape. We recommend doing it by day so you do not miss the views.

There is also a faster route via a smaller airport in Palopo, but connections are limited. Our guide covers all options with current prices and recommended operators.

Best time to visit

  • June–September: dry season, best for hiking
  • August: peak funeral season — most ceremonies
  • December–March: rainy but fewer tourists
  • Avoid: national holidays (very crowded)
